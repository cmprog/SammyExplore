# Story Bible: "This Is Not Your Story"

Stakeholder-approved premise (2026-10-11). Details below the "Approved" section are drafts for exploring the voice, and the stakeholder can change them.

## Approved

- **Sammy** is an owl and the hero, and a **boy**. Sammy never gets hurt and never dies.
  Family history worth playing with: for a long time Sammy was just called "an Owl", and only later did everyone settle on him being a boy.
- **The Narrator** is sure this game is the epic tale of a heroic **Eagle**, and keeps "correcting" the world to make it fit that story.
  Sammy keeps showing up anyway. Over the course of the game the narrator moves from denial, to grudging acceptance, to being fond of Sammy.
  The tone is Stanley Parable–style: the narrator comments on what the player does, reacts when the player ignores instructions, and subverts expectations.
- **The cats are not in real danger.** They're stuck in silly places.
  - **Twyx** (girl): a **tuxedo cat** (black with a white chest, paws, and muzzle) with green eyes. Sammy meets her early.
    She is very loving and protective, and she follows her favorite person everywhere. **She is the heart of this game, so treat her with special care.**
    She is always warm, loyal, and safe. Never put her in peril, never have her leave, get lost, or disappear, and never write goodbyes or sad beats for her.
    Jokes are fine; the narrator can be baffled by her, but never unkind.
  - **Snowy** (girl): a **black cat** (yes, named Snowy). Mischievous, and acts like a kitten even though she's an adult.
    She's fascinated by a wall-mounted clock with a bell chime, loves string toys, and loves being picked up so she can bat at the pull chains on ceiling fans.
    She has gotten her own collar stuck in her mouth more than once (a candidate idea for her Chapter Two predicament).
    She is the rescue in **Chapter Two**.
  - **Moxie**, **Elphaba** (both girls): cats to rescue. Their looks and personalities are still to be decided.
  - **Oshi** (boy, the only boy among the cats): a cat to rescue, and very large. The game calls him "the furry basketball" and makes other silly references to his size.
- **Night is the main setting.** Daytime can happen when the story calls for it.
- **Resets** happen when Sammy gets into a weird situation. The narrator "corrects" it by rewinding, rewriting, or putting Sammy back. A reset is never framed as death, failure, or harm.
- Narration appears as on-screen text.

## Structure (approved 2026-10-13; details still open)

- **Chapter One** (built): Sammy meets Twyx.
- Then a **home base**: a **girl's bedroom**. Pink walls, Minecraft-style decorations, Lego, and typical kid stuff. Rescued cats roam free there. Sammy goes there to "pick up" a cat to bring along.
- **One cat rescue per chapter.** Chapters are entered from the home base; how exactly is still open.
- **The companion follows Sammy.** Where she can't follow (a gap, a locked door, a ledge too high), Sammy solves a small mini-puzzle to help her through, for example lowering a platform or unlocking a door.
- **Cat spots:** marked places where a companion's ability can be used. A cat spot **does not show which cat** it needs. With no cat, or the wrong one, the narrator makes japes that work as soft hints. Controls stay simple: no new keys.
- **Cats have special abilities.** Rescuing a cat means getting past a challenge or obstacle, and choosing the right cat (and using her ability) is a key part of solving it. The details still need to be worked out with the stakeholder.

## Narrator voice (draft, M1 exploration)

- Grand, storybook, slightly pompous. Bothered by owls; adores eagles.
- Short lines that a 9-year-old reads quickly: one or two sentences, at most about 20 words each.
- Jokes come from the narrator's expectations failing ("The eagle soared... the *owl* soared. Fine. Close enough.").
- Reacts to what the player actually does: ignoring instructions, standing still, flapping too much, going the wrong way, pressing E on random things.
- Never mean to Sammy or the player. The narrator gets flustered and doesn't get cruel.
- Running gags are welcome: misnaming Sammy, an eagle cutout or eagle portrait the narrator keeps trying to place, the narrator's "script" showing through.
- Approved gag: the narrator's script has a cast list that says **"THE OWL (gender: Owl)"**, and the narrator gets flustered whenever Sammy is called "he".
